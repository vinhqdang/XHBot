\section{Numerical Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Every closed form in Section~\ref{sec:theory} is checked against Monte-Carlo
simulation of Definition~\ref{def:ccsbm}. Our purpose here is verification of the
analysis, not a performance claim: the results below establish that the derivations
are correct, and say nothing on their own about accuracy on real bot graphs.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} addresses that gap directly.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Unless stated otherwise we use $\pi_B = 0.3$ ($c \approx 0.4286$),
$\|\Delta\| = 2$, $\sigma = 1$, and $D = 16$, drawing $4\times 10^5$ nodes per class
per configuration ($1.5 \times 10^6$ for the crossover sweep). Errors are computed
by exhaustive threshold search over the empirical distributions, so the reported
figures are attained minima rather than plug-in estimates. All code is released
with the paper and runs in a few minutes on CPU.

Theorem~\ref{thm:error} against simulation. Separation matches to within
$1.2\times 10^{-3}$ and the standard deviation---including the mixture term that a

\subsection{The critical ratio}

The central prediction is Corollary~\ref{cor:critical}. Solving
$\mathrm{SNR}(\rho) = \|\Delta\|/(2\sigma)$ numerically at $D=16$ gives
$\rho^\star = 0.4644$. Sweeping $\rho$ in steps of $0.01$ with $1.5\times 10^6$
samples per point, aggregation beats raw features at $\rho = 0.46$
($\varepsilon = 0.1545$ against $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0.1587$) and loses at
$\rho = 0.47$ ($\varepsilon = 0.1648$). The measured crossover therefore lies in
$[0.46, 0.47]$, containing the predicted value.

Table~\ref{tab:critical} separates the two forms of the corollary. The exact
crossover differs from the closed form~\eqref{eq:critical} at moderate SNR, as
expected, since~\eqref{eq:critical} drops the mixture variance. Re-running the
exact solver in the noise-dominated regime it was derived for ($\|\Delta\| = 0.2$,
$\sigma = 1$) recovers the closed form to three decimal places at every degree,
confirming that the discrepancy is the stated approximation and not an error.

Figure~\ref{fig:critical} plots both curves against degree. The tolerated
camouflage rises steeply for sparsely connected accounts and flattens as $D$ grows,
which has a concrete operational reading: the accounts most vulnerable to
camouflage are those with few connections, and a deployment that aggregates over
small neighbourhoods is fragile in exactly the regime where bots are cheapest to
create.

\subsection{SGTR}

Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} predicts expected squared discrepancies of $2d\sigma^2$ and
$\|\Delta\|^2 + 2d\sigma^2$. At $d = 8$, $\sigma = 1$, $\|\Delta\| = 2$ these are
$16$ and $20$; simulation over $2\times10^5$ edge samples gives $16.005$ and
$20.005$, an error below $0.03\%$.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:sgtr} we verify the closed
form~\eqref{eq:rhostarsgtr} by round-trip: substituting
$\rho^\star_{\mathrm{SGTR}}$ back into $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ must return
$\rho^\star$. The maximum absolute deviation over
$\bar e_c \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9\}$ is $1.1\times 10^{-16}$, i.e.\ machine
precision. Table~\ref{tab:sgtr} reports the resulting tolerance gains, and
Figure~\ref{fig:sgtr} plots the full curve.

\subsection{THCA}

Theorem~\ref{thm:thca} is constructive and we verify both halves programmatically.
The neighbourhoods $\{0,2\}$ and $\{1,1\}$ have identical means ($1.0$ and $1.0$)
and distinct maxima ($2.0$ and $1.0$), so mean aggregation cannot separate them
while the max channel can; and setting $\alpha = (1,0,0)$ reproduces the homophilic
channel exactly. Both checks pass.
