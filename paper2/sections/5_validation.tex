\section{Numerical Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Every closed form in Section~\ref{sec:theory} is checked against Monte-Carlo
simulation of Definition~\ref{def:ccsbm}. Our purpose here is verification of the
analysis, not a performance claim: the results below establish that the derivations
are correct, and say nothing on their own about accuracy on real bot graphs.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} addresses that gap directly.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Unless stated otherwise we use $\pi_B = 0.3$ ($c \approx 0.4286$),
$\|\Delta\| = 2$, $\sigma = 1$, and $D = 16$, drawing $4\times 10^5$ nodes per class
per configuration ($1.5 \times 10^6$ for the crossover sweep). Errors are computed
by exhaustive threshold search over the empirical distributions, so the reported
figures are attained minima rather than plug-in estimates. All code is released
with the paper and runs in a few minutes on CPU.

\subsection{The critical ratio}

The central prediction is Corollary~\ref{cor:critical}. Solving
$\mathrm{SNR}(\rho) = \|\Delta\|/(2\sigma)$ numerically at $D=16$ gives
$\rho^\star = 0.4644$. Sweeping $\rho$ in steps of $0.01$ with $1.5\times 10^6$
samples per point, aggregation beats raw features at $\rho = 0.46$
($\varepsilon = 0.1545$ against $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0.1587$) and loses at
$\rho = 0.47$ ($\varepsilon = 0.1648$). The measured crossover therefore lies in
$[0.46, 0.47]$, containing the predicted value.

Table~\ref{tab:critical} separates the two forms of the corollary. The exact
crossover differs from the closed form~\eqref{eq:critical} at moderate SNR, as
expected, since~\eqref{eq:critical} drops the mixture variance. Re-running the
exact solver in the noise-dominated regime it was derived for ($\|\Delta\| = 0.2$,
$\sigma = 1$) recovers the closed form to three decimal places at every degree,
confirming that the discrepancy is the stated approximation and not an error.

Figure~\ref{fig:critical} plots both curves against degree. The tolerated
camouflage rises steeply for sparsely connected accounts and flattens as $D$ grows,
which has a concrete operational reading: the accounts most vulnerable to
camouflage are those with few connections, and a deployment that aggregates over
small neighbourhoods is fragile in exactly the regime where bots are cheapest to
create.

\subsection{SGTR}

Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} predicts expected squared discrepancies of $2d\sigma^2$ and
$\|\Delta\|^2 + 2d\sigma^2$. At $d = 8$, $\sigma = 1$, $\|\Delta\| = 2$ these are
$16$ and $20$; simulation over $2\times10^5$ edge samples gives $16.005$ and
$20.005$, an error below $0.03\%$.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:sgtr} we verify the closed
form~\eqref{eq:rhostarsgtr} by round-trip: substituting
$\rho^\star_{\mathrm{SGTR}}$ back into $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ must return
$\rho^\star$. The maximum absolute deviation over
$\bar e_c \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9\}$ is $1.1\times 10^{-16}$, i.e.\ machine
precision. Table~\ref{tab:sgtr} reports the resulting tolerance gains, and
Figure~\ref{fig:sgtr} plots the full curve.

\subsection{The extensions: achievable pruning and depth}
\label{sec:validation-ext}

The effective-degree formula~\eqref{eq:deff} matches Monte-Carlo variance to
within $0.5\%$ for $\theta \geq 0.5$. At $\theta = 0$ it is off by $7.3\%$
($8.00$ predicted against $7.41$ measured at $\rho=0.5$, $D=16$), and the cause is
not sampling: it is Jensen's inequality on $\mathbb{E}[1/K]$ for the random retained
count $K$, exactly the effect quantified in Remark~\ref{rem:no-ceiling}. Under
perfect pruning the variance-equivalent degree is $1.29$ rather than the $1.00$ that
$\mathbb{E}[K]$ predicts at $\rho = 1-1/D$, $D=16$, and $35.6\%$ of nodes retain no
neighbour at all; the proportion still benefiting falls to one half at
$\rho = 0.897$ ($D=16$) and $0.974$ ($D=64$).

Correcting $\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as in~\eqref{eq:pruned-condition} raises the
tolerances relative to the $c\,\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ substitution: at $D=16$,
$\pi_B=0.3$ we obtain $0.609$, $0.715$, $0.847$ and $0.928$ for
$\bar e_c = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9$, against $0.594$, $0.679$, $0.788$ and $0.869$
under the incorrect form. The error was therefore pessimistic, and by up to six
percentage points.

For depth, \texttt{theory/corrections.py} evaluates~\eqref{eq:depth-exact}
directly. The weight profile $\sum_j (q^{(L)}_j)^2/N_j$ exceeds the
non-backtracking value $D^{-L}$ by factors of $1.94$, $4.63$, $12.3$ and $35.0$ at
$L = 2,\dots,5$, which is the quantitative size of the term
that~\eqref{eq:backtrack} restores. The resulting crossovers are
$\rho^\star_1 = 0.5250$, $\rho^\star_2 = 0.5868$, $\rho^\star_3 = 0.5528$,
$\rho^\star_4 = 0.5756$ and $\rho^\star_5 = 0.5567$: every multi-layer value lies
above the single-layer one, so depth increases the tolerated camouflage, and the
crossover is \emph{not} depth-independent.

\subsection{Measuring the camouflage ratio on real graphs}
\label{sec:measuring}

The theory is parameterised by $\rho$, which has so far been a modelling device. We
now measure it. TwiBot-20 and TwiBot-22 require author approval for access, which we
did not have. We therefore use \textbf{MGTAB}~\cite{shi2023mgtab}, an openly
distributed Twitter dataset of $10{,}199$ expert-annotated accounts
($2{,}748$ automated, $7{,}451$ genuine; $c = 0.369$) in which \emph{every} account
carries a label, so that all ties are between labelled actors and the measurement is
not diluted by unlabelled alters. MGTAB records seven relations on the same
population, which lets us separate the social layer (following, friendship, mention,
reply, quote) from a content-coordination layer (shared URLs, shared hashtags). For
comparison we also report the two review networks of Dou
et al.~\cite{dou2020caregnn}; these are not social ties, and we treat them only as a
contrast case.

\paragraph{A negative result on the canonical benchmark.}
It is worth recording why we do not use cresci-2015 or cresci-2017, the
longest-standing bot benchmarks. In the openly distributed preprocessed release,
cresci-2017 contains \emph{no} interpersonal ties at all: all $6{,}637{,}615$ of its
edges are account-to-tweet postings. cresci-2015 does contain following and
friendship ties, but only $0.41\%$ of the $1.29$ million accounts they span are
labelled, and the labelled-to-labelled subgraph reduces to $4{,}275$ ties with the
mixing pattern $3{,}823$ genuine--genuine, $373$ automated--automated and just
$\mathbf{79}$ cross-class. Only $698$ of $3{,}351$ automated accounts ($20.8\%$) have
any labelled tie, the median such account has degree $0$, and the median camouflage
ratio is therefore $0$. A quantity resting on $79$ ties cannot support a
distributional claim, and we obtained the same counts from a second, independently
preprocessed release of the same data, so this is a property of the dataset rather
than of one pipeline. We report it because it explains a gap in the literature: the
benchmark most used for bot detection cannot be used to measure the structural
mechanism the field attributes to bots.

For each relation we compute $\rho$ as the mean over automated accounts of the
fraction of their alters that are genuine, and $\eta$ symmetrically.
Table~\ref{tab:real} reports the result.

Three findings, and one honest failure.

\paragraph{The layers behave completely differently, and the theory says which one
matters.} On every social relation the automated accounts are almost perfectly
camouflaged: $\tilde\rho = 1.00$, meaning the median automated account has
\emph{no} automated alter at all. Automated--automated ties number $212$ in the
following-and-friendship union against $40{,}263$ automated--genuine ties. On the
content-coordination relations the same accounts behave in the opposite way:
$\hat\rho$ falls to $0.60$--$0.63$ and automated--automated ties rise to $15{,}963$
(shared URL) and $28{,}203$ (shared hashtag). These accounts avoid each other where
ties are visible and converge where content is shared.

This is where the non-monotonicity of~\eqref{eq:window} does real work. Because
camouflage on the social layer is close to \emph{total}, those relations sit at or
beyond the \emph{upper} edge of the harmful window rather than inside it: a
neighbourhood that is $100\%$ genuine, in a population that is $27\%$ automated, is
itself anomalous, and relational inference is informative again with its sign
reversed. The relations that actually defeat inference are the content ones at
intermediate $\rho \approx 0.6$, which fall inside the window. The most damaging
strategy is not maximal camouflage but partial camouflage---and the accounts in this
population overshoot it on precisely the layer a detector is most likely to use.

\paragraph{Multiplexity, not density, decides usability.} The two layers are recorded
on the \emph{same} $10{,}199$ accounts, and $\hat\rho$ differs between them by a
factor approaching two, with the qualitative verdict differing as well. A procedure
that pools relations into one adjacency therefore averages a signal that is
informative-with-reversed-sign together with one that is genuinely uninformative. This
is the concrete form of the concern raised by V\"or\"os and
Snijders~\cite{voros2017cluster} and by Snijders et al.~\cite{snijders2013model}: the
quantity is a property of a relation, not of a population.

\paragraph{The edge-balance identity requires its degree correction here, and is
still only approximate.} On the social union, $\eta = c\rho$ predicts $0.365$ against
a measured $0.032$---wrong by a factor of $11$. The reason is visible in the degrees:
automated accounts have mean degree $14.8$ against $83.5$ for genuine ones, a ratio of
$0.18$, so the regular-degree assumption behind~\eqref{eq:balance} fails badly. The
degree-corrected form $\eta = c\rho\,(\bar d_B/\bar d_H)$ gives $0.067$, within a
factor of two but still not close. We take this as the honest boundary of the
identity: it holds to within $3\%$ on YelpChi, where class degrees are comparable, and
degrades in proportion to degree asymmetry. Since $\eta$ is a mean of per-node ratios
rather than a ratio of means, no single scalar correction will recover it under strong
within-class degree skew, and the identity should be read as exact in the model and
diagnostic---not predictive---on data.

\paragraph{Caveat.}
The window is derived for a CSBM with regular degrees, isotropic Gaussian features,
and a single relation. Real graphs violate all three: degrees are heavy-tailed,
features are neither Gaussian nor isotropic, and the relations interact. The
numbers above should be read as locating these graphs approximately, not as a
certified verdict, and the agreement of $\eta$ with $c\rho$ on YelpChi is the one
quantity here that constitutes a genuine test of the theory rather than an
application of it. Measuring $\rho$ on TwiBot remains the natural next step and
requires only data access; \texttt{theory/measure\_rho.py} implements the estimator
and validates it against planted ground truth, recovering $\rho$ exactly and
confirming $\eta = c\rho$ on synthetic graphs with regular degrees.
